# ١١ — Instrumentation

## Definition

The work does not specify a fixed ensemble. The instrumentation is determined by:

1. The strata being inhabited.
2. The domains being foregrounded.
3. The gestures being initiated.
4. The performances available.
5. The acoustics of the performance space.
6. The performers' choices.

The score lists *instrumental families* and *instrumental behaviors* that may be used. The performers select from these.

The work also includes instruments that are *not normally considered instruments* — silence instruments, memory instruments, theft instruments, structure instruments.

---

## Instrumental Families

### Family 1 — Voices

The work uses human voices in multiple registers and modes:

- **Speaking voice** (for the Stratum of the Scribe, the Wise, the Merchant, and for theft documentation).
- **Singing voice** (for the Lover, the Priest, sometimes the Wise).
- **Whispered voice** (for the Scribe's commentary, for the Dying's fragments).
- **Children's voice** (for the Stratum of the Child).
- **Breath voice** (for the Dying, for silences in Stage 2, 4, 5).
- **Crowd voice** (when multiple performers are present and speak/sing/whisper together).

The voice is the work's primary instrument because it is the instrument of memory, forgetting, and theft. The voice can remember what it has heard. The voice can forget what it has said. The voice can steal from other voices.

---

### Family 2 — Strings

The work uses strings in their extended ranges:

- **Low strings** (cello, double bass) — for the Priest, the Structure.
- **Middle strings** (viola, violin) — for the Wise, the Merchant, the Lover.
- **High strings** (violin harmonics, optionally a high-register instrument) — for the Child.

The strings are used in their full range of techniques:

- Sustained tones (Lover, Priest).
- Pizzicato (Merchant, Wise).
- Col legno (Wise, Structure).
- Sul ponticello (Child, sometimes Lover).
- Sul tasto (Priest, Lover).
- Harmonics (Child, Lover).
- Glissandi (Merchant, Mad).
- Multiphonics (not applicable to conventional strings — see extended techniques).
- Bowed metal objects (substitute for strings when the work requires it).

---

### Family 3 — Winds

The work uses winds in their extended ranges:

- **Low winds** (bass clarinet, contrabassoon, low brass) — for the Priest, the Structure.
- **Middle winds** (clarinet, oboe, horn, trumpet) — for the Wise, the Merchant, the Scribe (occasionally).
- **High winds** (flute, piccolo, high-register woodwinds) — for the Child.
- **Microtonal winds** — instruments capable of quarter-tones and eighth-tones (some Middle Eastern winds, some modified Western winds) — for the Lover.

The winds are used in their full range of techniques, including:

- Extended techniques (multiphonics, key-clicks, breath sounds, overblown tones).
- Microtonal inflections.
- Circular breathing (for sustained strata).

---

### Family 4 — Percussion

The work uses percussion in three categories:

- **Tuned percussion** (vibraphone, marimba, glockenspiel, tubular bells tuned to mathematical ratios) — for the Priest, the Wise, the Structure.
- **Untuned percussion** (bass drum, snare, tom-toms, woodblocks, cymbals) — for the Wise, the Mad, the Merchant.
- **Small percussion** (toy instruments, shakers, bells, whistles, children's instruments) — for the Child, the Scribe (occasionally).

The work also uses:

- **Body percussion** (hand claps, finger snaps, body strikes, breath) — for the Mad, the Child.
- **Found percussion** (any object struck, scraped, or shaken) — for the Mad, the Merchant, theft documentation.

---

### Family 5 — Electronics

The work uses electronics in limited, specific ways:

- **Analog electronics** (sine-tone generators, oscillators, modular synthesizers) — for the Structure.
- **Sampling/Playback** — for memory instruments (see below).
